Varieties of Democracy (V-Dem) goes beyond asking whether a country holds elections. Its data measure many dimensions of democracy and autocracy, so that countries can be compared with the same yardstick over more than two centuries. The V-Dem Institute at the University of Gothenburg leads the project, and the annual Democracy Report shows where democracy is advancing and where it is under pressure.

Why it matters here

V-Dem works through nine regional centres, from the Balkans and the Caucasus to Latin America and Southern Africa. This launch brings Australia and New Zealand into the network. Our region combines long-established democracies, very different political systems among our neighbours in the Asia-Pacific, and close ties with Europe, an ideal setting for comparative research.

A joint centreThe V-Dem Regional Centre for Australia and New Zealand is run jointly by the Australian National University and the University of Melbourne.

What it opens up

  • 1Compare Australia and New Zealand with democracies and autocracies worldwide, using the same measures
  • 2A regional network of country experts and researchers
  • 3Data training, workshops and visiting scholars for students and early-career researchers
  • 4Joint research and funding, including EU and Australia projects on AI and democratic resilience
  • 5Evidence for policymakers, journalists and civil society
